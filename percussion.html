Instrument group

Percussion

Percussion is more than drums. Some instruments keep the beat. Others have bars you play with mallets, and those can play real melodies.

Snare drum Suspended cymbal Marimba Xylophone Vibraphone Concert bass drum

Snare drum

The snare drum is a shallow drum with wires, called snares, stretched under the bottom head. You play it with sticks. It does not play the tune. It plays the beat, the accents, and the rolls that make a march feel like a march. In concert band it sits on a stand.

How it sounds

Crisp, dry, and sharp. One tap is a clean click with a short buzz from the snares. A roll is a smooth purr. It is the engine of the rhythm.

Suspended cymbal

A suspended cymbal is one cymbal hanging from a stand. It is not a pair of cymbals clapped together. You play it with yarn mallets or a stick. Players roll on it so the sound grows, or strike it once and let it ring.

How it sounds

A metallic shimmer. A soft roll starts as a whisper and can grow into a wash of sound. A hard strike is a bright splash that rings, then slowly fades. It is the sound of suspense before a big hit.

Marimba

The marimba is a large keyboard of wooden bars, with tubes called resonators hanging underneath. You play it with yarn or rubber mallets, often holding two or four. The bars are wide, and the notes last. It can play melodies and chords, not just rhythm.

How it sounds

Warm, round, and woody. A note blooms and hangs in the air. Chords sound like a soft choir. It is one of the most singing instruments in the percussion section.

Xylophone

The xylophone is also wooden bars played with mallets, but the bars are harder and narrower, and they sit higher than a marimba. Players usually use harder mallets. The notes are short, which makes fast lines stay clear.

How it sounds

Bright, dry, and percussive, like tuned wood that speaks and then stops. Next to a marimba, the xylophone is the sharper, shorter one. It sparkles above the band.

Vibraphone

The vibraphone has metal bars, tubes underneath, and a motor. The motor spins small fans so the sound gently pulses. That pulse is called vibrato. A foot pedal lets notes keep ringing, a lot like the sustain pedal on a piano.

How it sounds

Cool, glassy, and a little dreamy. With the motor on, each note wavers. Jazz players use it all the time. In band it adds a color you cannot get from wood.

Concert bass drum

The concert bass drum is a very large drum on a stand, played with a big soft beater. It is different from a marching bass drum that someone wears. One well-placed note can hold up a whole phrase.

How it sounds

A deep boom you feel in your chest. Soft hits are a heartbeat under the band. Loud hits are thunder at a climax. It gives the music weight.
