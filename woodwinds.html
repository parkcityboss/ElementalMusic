Instrument group

Woodwinds

Woodwind players blow air across a hole or through a reed. They carry a lot of the melody. Saxophones are metal, but they use a reed, so they sit in this group.

Clarinet

The clarinet is a long black tube, usually wood or plastic, with a single reed on the mouthpiece. The reed is a thin slice of cane that vibrates when you blow. Clarinets can play from very low to very high, and most bands have a lot of them. Plenty of beginners start here.

How it sounds

Low notes are dark and warm. The middle is smooth. High notes are bright. It can whisper or cut through. A section of clarinets together sounds like a choir in one color.

Flute

The flute is a metal tube you hold sideways. You blow across a hole, the same idea as blowing across a bottle. There is no reed. It is light to carry and plays the highest woodwind notes. Older flutes were wood, which is why a metal flute still counts as a woodwind.

How it sounds

Clear, light, and quick. High notes sparkle. Low notes are softer and a little hollow. Fast runs sound like birds. One quiet flute can float over the whole band.

Oboe

The oboe is a narrow wooden tube with a double reed: two small pieces of cane tied together. You blow between them. It takes steady air, so it is usually not the very first instrument a brand-new player starts on, but every band wants an oboe. Before a concert, the oboe plays the tuning note so everyone can match pitch.

How it sounds

Sweet, a little nasal, and easy to pick out of a crowd. Long notes seem to sing. It can sound lonely or tender. Even when the band is loud, an oboe line stays clear.

Alto saxophone

The alto saxophone is made of brass, but it uses a single reed, so it sits with the woodwinds. The body curves, and the neck bends toward the player. It was built for bands. It plays in the middle of the group, and it is also the saxophone people know from jazz.

How it sounds

Reedy and flexible. It can blend with the clarinets or step forward with a cooler edge. It gets loud with less effort than a clarinet. Melodies sound smooth. Rhythm parts sound punchy.

Tenor saxophone

The tenor saxophone is larger than the alto and plays lower. It has the same reed and the same curved shape, with a longer neck. In band it often plays inner harmonies, countermelodies, and solos.

How it sounds

Darker and warmer than the alto. Low notes are full. The middle can sound smoky. If the alto is the brighter voice, the tenor is the deeper one beside it.
