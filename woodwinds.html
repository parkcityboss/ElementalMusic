Instrument group

Woodwinds

Woodwind players blow air over a reed or across a hole. They are the choir of the band and helo the band sound full. While not all woodwind instruments are made of wood, they are all considered woodwind instruments because of the way they sound, and their role in the band

Clarinet Flute Oboe Alto saxophone Tenor saxophone

Clarinet

The clarinet is a long black tube, usually wood or plastic, with a single reed on the mouthpiece. The reed is a thin slice of cane that vibrates when you blow. Clarinets can play from very low to very high, and most bands have a lot of them. Plenty of beginners start here.

How it sounds

Low notes are dark and warm. High notes are bright. It can whisper or cut through. In a section, Clarinets have a beautiful sound that is simmilar to a section of violins in an orchestra.

Flute

The flute is a metal tube you hold sideways. You blow across a hole, almost like blowing across the top of a bottle. It usually plays the highest notes in the woodwind section.

How it sounds

Clear, and light like a feather. Low notes are softer and airy. High notes sing across the top of the band. Flutes are like the cherry on top of a full band sound

Oboe

The oboe is a narrow wooden tube with a double reed: two small pieces of cane tied together. You blow between them. It takes steady air, so it is usually not the very first instrument a brand-new player starts on. Usually a clarinet player will transition to the oboe.

How it sounds

Simmilar to a clarinet while being a bit brighter and more forward. The sound of the oboe cuts through the band and is often used to play melodies that are easy for listeners to hear among the sound of the rest of the band.

Alto saxophone

The alto saxophone is made of brass, but sound is created using a reed, so it is considered a woodwind instrument. The body curves, and the neck bends toward the player. The Saxophone is most well known in Jazz music.

How it sounds

The alto saxophone sounds simmilar to the clarient but has a bolder and wider sound. It can blend in with the rest of the woodwind section or play out for the listener to hear.

Tenor saxophone

The tenor saxophone is larger than the alto and plays lower. It has the same reed and the same curved shape, with a longer neck.

How it sounds

Darker and warmer than the alto Saxophone. Low notes sound full and heavy. The tenor saxophone is used to support the sound of the rest of the woodwind section.
